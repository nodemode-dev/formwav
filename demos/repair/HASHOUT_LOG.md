## v2.23.3 first version

- Focus, from the demo brief: repair and master one real noisy recording, with the spectral view, noise reduction, de-hum, de-click, EQ, compression, loudness to a target and a before and after A/B. The first demo already shows destructive per-track edits and a mix; this one is one recording on a live chain, which is how the app keeps repair (live effect kinds `denoise`, `dehum`, `declick`, `compressor`, `limiter` and the filters in `frontend/src/engine/document.ts`).
- Shape: a bench, not a multitrack. The rack runs in the app's order (heal, de-click, de-hum, reduce noise, EQ, compressor, loudness and limiter) over the original, and each stage is cached on its input identity and its parameters, so moving the compressor does not re-run the de-click. The first render of the 24 s One-Step measured 868 ms in headless Chrome and 3510 ms with the CPU throttled 4x (the status bar prints it); the page marks the title "busy" meanwhile instead of rebuilding anything.
- Heal follows CLAUDE.md. A drag on a spectrogram only draws a box; the Heal selection button adds it to the Heal stage; nothing heals on the drag, and there is no brush. The default source for a healed cell is the surround ("surround" in `backend/app/services/effects.py`): the box is rebuilt from the magnitudes in the frames just before and after it, interpolated across it, and only ever lowered (`min(current, reference)`), with a three-bin taper at the box edges and the original phase kept. The "inside" fallback is not carried.
- De-click: a 12th-order linear predictor per 1024-sample block gives a residual; samples whose residual passes sensitivity times a robust sigma (a histogram median of the residual) are hits; hits within 16 samples merge into a span, widened by 10 samples before and 14 after, and spans longer than the chosen maximum (default 1 ms) are left alone as music. A span is rebuilt by predicting forward from before it and backward from after it and cross-fading the two. The pops on a 78 are 16 to 40 samples wide smooth bumps, so a pass that treats only the samples that cross the threshold leaves the bump's shoulders; the stage therefore runs three passes. Measured on the One-Step with the stage's defaults: 83 clicks found, 18 left; on the Caruso clip 129 found, 3 left. A first version that did one pass on the bare hit samples changed about 4 samples per click and left 94 of 91; that and an unbuilt edit of `fx.ts` made the demo report more clicks after repair than before, which is why the stage now reports from `left`, the clicks a fresh detector still finds in the stage's output.
- "Clicks found" for the repaired recording is that `left` count (inside the selected range when there is one), because the same detector on the final output counts noise-reduction artefacts and gain, not pops: a quieter background lowers the local sigma and makes untouched ripples look like clicks.
- The hum row is "Hum below level" for the same reason (the hum energy at 50 or 60 Hz and three harmonics, as a gap to the loudness), where the absolute figure rose 7.5 dB on the One-Step. The mains frequency is chosen once per recording, 50 or 60 Hz by whichever fundamental is stronger in the original, and both sides are measured at that choice; the first version took the louder of the two sets of harmonics on each side, and on the hum reading that picked the 50 Hz set (speech partials at 150 and 200 Hz) so the notched 60 Hz hum did not show. Measured on the hum reading with the stage's preset: 37.7 dB below the level before, 53.6 dB after. The Clicks row reads "De-click off" for the repaired side when that stage is bypassed.
- The Measure row is "Noise below level" (loudness minus the quietest tenth of 50 ms blocks) instead of the absolute noise floor. The loudness stage raises the whole recording by about 8 dB, so the absolute floor rose by 7 dB after a repair that had lowered it, and the table read as a failure. As a gap to the level it is gain-proof: 5.3 dB before, 6.5 dB after on the One-Step; 30 dB on the hum reading after the notches.
- Loudness: BS.1770 from the first demo's `dsp.ts`; the master stage finds the gain to the target, applies it, and a look-ahead limiter holds the ceiling (default -1 dBFS). The exported WAV of the One-Step measures -16.0 LUFS and a -1.0 dBFS peak with ffmpeg's ebur128, which is the check that the number on the screen is the number in the file.
- Compressor: a soft-knee (6 dB) downward compressor on a peak envelope with attack and release smoothing; the gain reduction curve is kept, in 10 ms blocks, for the Dynamics tab. The limiter looks 5 ms ahead and releases over 120 ms.
- A and B are two sources started together at the same offset, each behind its own gain node; the switch moves the gains with an 8 ms time constant so it does not click and the position never moves. Match level subtracts the loudness difference from the repaired side so a louder file does not sound better by being louder.
- Sources: the One-Step and the Caruso are Wikimedia Commons public-domain files cut to 24 s and 26 s as mono mp3 packs (a mono pack halves the size and the bench is mono). The hum reading and the NASA launch commentary are read from the first demo's packs by relative path rather than copied, so the media budget counts them once. Each source carries its own chain preset in `bench.ts`: the shellac gets more reduction and a duller top, the hum reading gets five notches at 60 Hz, the radio gets no de-click.
- Zero flash: the page is static markup patched in place; the stage rows, canvases and the measure table cells exist from the first paint and change text only; the measure table is fixed layout with set column widths so a longer note cannot move a column; the theme boots inline before the first paint. A 4x CPU throttled load with a throttled network measured a total layout shift of 0.0004 (sub-pixel row heights in the Measure table before the fixed layout; 0.0004 after it).
- Not done: no stereo, no saving of the chain as a preset, no per-range chain, no encoding other than WAV, and no hosted plug-ins.
