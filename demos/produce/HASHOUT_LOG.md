## v2.23.3 first version

- Focus, from the demo brief: music and sound design, a beat or score built from real CC0 or public-domain stems and one-shots, effects chains, automation, markers and a bounce. The first demo edits recordings destructively on one buffer per track; this one is clips on tracks in bars and beats with live chains, which is how the app arranges music. Features were checked against the app before they went in: the step sequencer (`frontend/src/ui/step-sequencer.ts`: 16 steps, a velocity per step, swing 50 to 75 on the off-beat sixteenths), the effect cards and their knob (`ui/editor-tabs.ts`, `ui/knob.ts`), automation lanes per track and per filter parameter (`engine/document.ts laneAt`), markers with colour and comment (`ui/labels-pane.ts`), and bounce (`ui/editing.ts bounceTrack`, Export). Left out because the app's versions need the engine or the Rust core: gate, de-noise and repair kinds (the second demo), sends and buses, VCAs, hosted plug-ins, tempo changes, time stretch and MIDI.
- Story: a 32 bar piece at 130 BPM, "Cold Open". The Satie piano and the NASA Saturn plasma waves open it (bars 1 to 5); the piano fades out across bars 4 to 6 while a low-pass on the Saturn bed and another on the drums open through the build (bars 5 to 9); a first drop (bar 9) plays the drums, bass and keys stems with a sequenced groove on top; a break (bar 17) leaves the keys stem alone, with a one-bar kit loop on a high-passed Perc track from bar 13; the launch roar rises from bar 23 into the second drop (bar 25); the last bars bring in a second kit loop whose pan sweeps from right to left. The seven markers (Cold open, Build, Drop A, Break, Riser, Drop B, Outro) carry the section names and comments.
- Stems, not songs: the CC0 item carries a 130 BPM techno track as four stems (bass, drums, keys and a vocal pella); the pella is not used. Eight-bar cuts at bar lines (1.846 s a bar) lay on the grid with no stretching: drums at bars 25, 57 and 73, bass at 57 and 73, keys at 25, 41, 57 and 73.- One-shots are sliced from the kit loops of the second CC0 item, by hand-picked onset times, with a 12 ms fade out and a 24-sample fade in; the eight sit as the sequencer's rows. A pattern renders in the browser by mixing those one-shots per step with velocity and swing into a stereo buffer, which becomes a library item and a clip.
- One graph, two uses: `audio.ts` builds clip sources, per-track chain (filters, compressor, reverb with a generated impulse response (decaying noise, 0.35 s plus 2.8 s times Room long, damped by a one-pole low-pass), echo with feedback, chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo, limiter), fader, pan and a master limiter, once, for live playback and for an `OfflineAudioContext` bounce. Automation is scheduled onto the nodes' parameters in both (pan and effect parameters linear, filter frequency exponential, volume in dB points scheduled every sixteenth so it ramps as a level), so what plays is what is saved.
- The limiter is the browser's compressor node set to ratio 20, knee 0, attack 2 ms, so it is not a brick wall: the opening mix measures -13.8 LUFS with a -0.2 dBFS peak. The session's master is therefore set to -6 dB; at -3 dB the same mix peaked at +0.5 dBFS and a 24-bit WAV of it clipped. The bounce dialog's loudness target scales the render and caps the gain so the peak stays at or under -1 dBFS (it says so when it does): asking for -14 LUFS on the whole piece gives -14.7 LUFS and a -1.0 dBFS peak, both measured on the saved file with ffmpeg's ebur128.
- Loudness is the first demo's BS.1770 code (`demo/src/dsp.ts`), read by relative path; `wav.ts` writes the 16, 24 or 32-bit float file.
- Edits are snapshots of the whole document (the arrays are small), 120 deep, so undo and redo cover clip moves, trims, splits, effects, knob moves, automation points, markers, sequencer patterns and bounces. A knob drag emits a live event while it moves and one history entry on release.
- Zero flash: the page is static markup patched in place; track rows, canvases and the dock panels exist from the first paint; the arrangement says "Loading the session media: n of 23" in its reserved space. The first build of this demo measured a layout shift of 0.091 under a 4x CPU throttle, because the Sounds groups and the effect row filled in after the packs decoded and pushed their neighbours; both now stay hidden (`visibility`, so their space is kept) until the session is built. The same run now measures 0.0001. The ruler row is 46 px so the marker flags have room, and the dock is 292 px so a card's knobs, the 8 sequencer rows and the mixer strips fit at 1440 by 900 without a scrollbar.
- Decode order: the packs decode in parallel and register in a fixed order afterwards, otherwise the Sounds list shows them in the order the decoder finished (Drums A, C, B).
- The status bar's mix reading runs the offline render 1.6 s after an edit and not during playback, so it never competes with the audio thread.
- Not done: no tempo map, no time stretch, no MIDI, no sends, no hosted plug-ins, no saving of the session, and no encoding other than WAV. A whole-arrangement bounce took between 6 and 29 s depending on what else the machine was running.
